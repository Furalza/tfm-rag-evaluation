ANSWER:
Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, like GraphRAG and its derivatives, offer significant advantages over vector-based RAG systems, especially in handling multi-hop reasoning. Below are the key ways they enhance this capability:

### 1. **Structured Reasoning with Entity-Relation Graphs**
Graph-based RAG systems utilize entity-relation graphs, which provide a structured representation of knowledge. This structure allows for systematic reasoning over multi-hop paths, meaning that these systems can efficiently link multiple pieces of evidence across various entities. Unlike vector-based systems, which rely on similarity measures in high-dimensional space, graph-based systems can track the relationships between entities explicitly, making it easier to navigate complex connections.

### 2. **Promoting Bridge Documents**
In multi-hop reasoning, particularly when addressing complex queries, identifying bridge documents—intermediate facts that connect disparate entities—is critical. Graph-based systems excel at this by organizing evidence in a graph structure, allowing for iterative retrieval that can surface bridge documents to leading positions. This iterative approach contrasts with vector-based RAG systems, which may overlook critical links due to their reliance on static document retrieval metrics.

### 3. **Iterative Retrieval Techniques**
Graph-based RAG systems implement iterative retrieval methods that perform multiple rounds of evidence collection. This process enhances the quality of retrieved information, ensuring that relevant evidence can be refined and re-ranked through successive rounds. Such techniques are particularly beneficial for multi-hop questions where bridging diverse data points is necessary. In comparison, traditional vector-based systems typically operate on a single retrieval pass, limiting their ability to refine results dynamically.

### 4. **Handling Complex Query Types**
Empirical findings suggest that graph-based systems, particularly those utilizing iterative retrieval, demonstrate substantial performance improvements on multi-hop questions, especially bridge-type queries. In these scenarios, the graph structure facilitates the identification and linking of relevant evidence that would generally be opaque within vector-based systems, which might provide no improvement or even degrade performance when tackling intricately connected questions.

### 5. **Complementary Evidence Capture**
Graph-based RAG systems often employ multiple iterative strategies that can capture complementary evidence across different retrieval attempts. This flexibility allows for a broader scope of evidence retrieval when addressing complex queries, something that vector-based RAG systems lack due to their more linear retrieval approach.

### Conclusion
The combination of structured graph representations, iterative retrieval, and enhanced ability to connect and navigate evidence makes graph-based RAG systems a superior option for multi-hop reasoning compared to vector-based approaches. Their design inherently supports complex reasoning tasks by focusing on relational context rather than merely vector similarity.
